# Actual footage vs illustrative footage

This is the distinction the whole feature exists for, and the one it would be
easiest to get wrong.

- **ACTUAL FOOTAGE** — the media genuinely depicts **the specific event the
  narration is describing**: the right event, the right place, the right date.
  Molotov signing on 23 August 1939, filmed on 23 August 1939.
- **ILLUSTRATIVE FOOTAGE** — real media that is **not of this event**. Real
  German soldiers, real 1939, wrong border crossing. Or a 2013 photograph of a
  museum replica standing in for an 1886 machine.
- **ARCHIVAL FOOTAGE / ARCHIVAL PHOTO** — real material from an archive, with no
  claim either way about the event.
- **AI GENERATED** — the model made it.
- **AI RECONSTRUCTION** — the model made it, and it depicts a real past event.

**Illustrative footage must never be presented as actual footage.** Everything
below is machinery for that one sentence.

## Why nothing automatic may claim ACTUAL FOOTAGE

Three signals are available at selection time, and not one of them establishes
an event match:

- **The ranker's relevance score.** It is a judgement about a search result — how
  well an asset answers a query — not evidence that the asset shows the event.
- **The archive's date field.** Measured, not assumed: Wikimedia Commons dated a
  1969 NASA clip `2015-06-12` (its YouTube upload) and answered "Benz
  Patent-Motorwagen 1886" with 2013 and 2021 photographs of museum **replicas**.
  The field is stored verbatim and shown labelled "dated"; it is never a source
  for the watermark.
- **Visual similarity.** It says two pictures look alike. Two newsreels of two
  different border crossings in 1939 look extremely alike.

So `classifyVisualOrigin` tops out at `archival_footage` / `archival_photo`, and
its confidence is capped at 85 — below `ACTUAL_FOOTAGE_MIN_CONFIDENCE` (90), so
that no automatic number can be mistaken for authority about the world.

`actual_footage` and `illustrative_footage` are reachable **only** through the
Footage type control, by a person, and choosing one records
`provenance_manually_verified = true` with confidence 100.

## Why an AI picture can never be promoted

`refuseFootageType` refuses every real origin on a scene whose `visual_source`
is `ai`, with the specification's own sentence:

> This visual was generated by AI. It cannot be marked as authentic footage
> unless the underlying media source has been replaced.

It is a **refusal, not a warning with a confirm button**. No confirmation makes
the bytes on screen anything other than what the model produced. The door is the
one the sentence names: replace the media. The archive panel sits directly under
the picture on the same step, and using an asset from it carries that asset's
own provenance across in the same transaction.

The mirror case is refused too — a real archive picture may not be relabelled as
AI — with "Back to AI" named as the honest way to do it.

## What "unknown" is for

A picture whose origin nothing reliable is known about reads **SOURCE
UNVERIFIED**, never nothing and never ACTUAL FOOTAGE. On a documentary, silence
about where a picture came from reads as a claim that it is real, so the label
is preferred over an empty corner. A producer upload, when that path exists,
defaults to `real_stock` or `unknown` — never to anything authentic.

## If a future run wants to propose ACTUAL FOOTAGE

It would need three separate judgements from the ranker — event match, location
match, date match — and `isExactEventMatch` is their conjunction. That is a
change to the `Archive Suggestions` workflow's rank prompt plus a store-stage
field, and even then the sensible design is for it to raise confidence and
*suggest* the promotion to the producer rather than take it. Nothing in the
current pipeline emits those three booleans, which is why the honest answer
today is that only a person can say ACTUAL FOOTAGE.
