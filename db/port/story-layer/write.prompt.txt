HOW A STORY IS TOLD HERE — these rules are what separate a film from a list of facts:
1. ONE THROUGHLINE. Every beat moves the protagonist toward or away from what they want. If a sentence does not change the situation, cut it.
2. SAY EVERYTHING ONCE. A fact, a date, a number, a character's introduction, a place's description, an image — each is stated exactly one time in the whole film. Later chapters never recap, never re-establish, never "return to" earlier material; they may refer back in three or four words at most. Re-read your draft for repeats before you finish.
3. EVENTS, NOT INVENTORY. Show the world through what people DO in it — decisions, failures, discoveries, confrontations, small victories — never through lists of objects ("Green felt. Brass ashtrays. A wall clock."). At most one short descriptive sentence per chapter; everything else is somebody doing something.
4. CAUSE AND EFFECT. Consecutive beats connect by "because" and "so", never by "and then". The end of each beat creates the need for the next.
5. CONCRETE AND VISUALIZABLE — real actions, places, people, moments. No abstract commentary ("that is the correction", "this is how power starts", "in many ways"), no lessons stated mid-story: the meaning is carried by the events. If the genre wants a lesson said aloud, it is said once, at the very end.
6. SENTENCES of 8-20 words — complete, flowing, spoken. Write in BEATS of ~20-22 words (one sentence, or a pair of short ones), because the text is cut into 8-second scenes; an idea that needs several beats keeps them consecutive so it never breaks mid-scene.
7. PROGRESSION. Time and place move forward and the viewer always knows when and where they are; no chapter re-opens in the moment a previous chapter opened.
8. Flowing narration only — no headings other than the chapter markers, no scene numbers, no meta text.
